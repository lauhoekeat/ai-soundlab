# Product and teaching plan

## Outcome and audience

Help a Year 4 pupil move from recognizing sounds to explaining physical causes, evaluating evidence and proposing a context-sensitive solution to noise. Working assumption: ages 10–11, BM as primary interface language, one school laptop per pair or small group, teacher-led lesson. Chinese support is an optional later addition, not a hidden MVP commitment. Owner confirms school stream and actual lesson language before content freeze.

Problem hypothesis: pupils may memorize definitions while finding it difficult to connect a sound to vibration, distinguish propagation from reflection, or explain why the same sound may help in one setting and disturb in another. This is a design hypothesis, not established classroom research. Validate with the teacher and a small, permissioned pupil walkthrough.

North-star evidence: the pupil produces a claim supported by an observation and explains one limitation of the AI. Operational targets: at least 4 of 5 pilot pairs complete a core mission with no more than one adult prompt; a 5-item paired pre/post check shows a median gain of at least one item. A small uncontrolled pilot supports iteration, not a claim of causal learning effectiveness.

## Story and four missions

At Sekolah Harmoni, a missing sound map has confused the preparations for Hari STEM. A friendly assistant, BIP, asks pupil detectives to restore four evidence files. BIP offers hypotheses and may be uncertain; pupils decide using evidence. No villains, surveillance or identifying people by voice.

| Mission | Story objective | Pupil action and evidence | Completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Jejak Getaran | Identify the laboratory's mystery signal | Predict clap/tap/paper-rustle; make the sound; inspect AI suggestion; observe a safe physical vibration demonstration; select cause and explain | Sound attempt or sample examined, vibration evidence recorded, explanation submitted |
| M2 Peta Perjalanan Bunyi | Reconnect listeners around the courtyard | Place listener markers around a source; predict who hears it; run a clearly labelled propagation simulation; revise prediction | Test at least three directions and explain why sound is heard around the source |
| M3 Rahsia Gema | Resolve the hall's returning signal | Compare a direct sample with a delayed repeat; change one virtual surface; predict, compare and identify a daily-life reflection example | Two conditions compared, one observation and one reflection example recorded |
| M4 Selamatkan Zon Tenang | Prepare a quiet reading area while preserving warnings | Classify contextual scene cards as useful/disturbing/context-dependent; choose two practical interventions; compare an illustrative before/after scene; justify | Two justified interventions and a short case report |

Every mission uses predict → act → observe → explain → revise. Recognition never awards science credit by itself. M1 delivers the core ML experience; M2–M4 are deterministic inquiry tasks with reviewed, rule-based feedback, not separate AI claims. Optional measured microphone exploration is outside MVP; no claim that the microphone detects reflection, vibration or direction.

## Scope

Must: onboarding and audio check, four missions, uncertainty and sample paths, BM copy, evidence notebook, local resume/reset, teacher review, printable case report, transparent AI/mode indicators and presenter jump controls.

Should only after musts pass: Chinese glossary, richer illustration, teacher aggregation from manually imported anonymous exports. Could later: student training activity. Out: generative chatbot, speech transcription, cloud accounts, live class dashboard, leaderboards, AR, school integration, personalized AI grading, real acoustic simulation, sound-level health assessment.

Explicit fallback: a locally inferred prerecorded example remains real inference but must read “AI pada sampel contoh”; a fixed prerecorded result is “Rakaman demonstrasi”; a rule result is “Maklum balas berasaskan peraturan.” A fallback-only artifact may teach sound but is not evidence that live AI passed the competition POC.

## Proposed 40-minute lesson

3 min story/pre-check; 3 min setup; 7 min M1; 5 min M2; 5 min M3; 7 min M4; 5 min report/post-check; 5 min discussion and buffer. In a crowded room, one group makes a sound at a time; other groups use samples and keep speakers muted during capture. Rotate operator, observer and reporter roles. No pupil must provide voice recordings.

Teacher value: sees the prediction, evidence, explanation, hints and revisions for each mission; chooses the next question rather than accepting an automatic mastery label. Learner value: short instructions, visible progress and useful recovery after an uncertain AI result.

## Success and scope control

Release requires all four mission flows, a measured AI gate result, teacher content signoff, no critical/high defects, tested offline presenter mode and reproducible evidence. If time slips, remove optional language and visual polish before removing a mission. If a mission must be removed, revise the product claim and obtain owner approval; do not ship an incomplete four-mission claim.
